# Segmentation benchmark, 2026-10-04

Generated by `tarn bench segment`. Each sample booklet's cached OCR is segmented against its seeded paper and compared with a labelling of where each answer starts (per page: the questions that start there, in written order, and the question the top of the page continues). The report holds question labels and counts only.

Metrics: **order** = the answers in written order (`?` = unassigned), exact or not, and the edit distance to the truth; **starts** = (page, question) pairs, precision and recall; **continuations** = pages whose top continues an answer, given the right one; **page Jaccard** = per page, overlap of the questions present (started or continued), averaged.

## Notes

- Truth of b-ci1, b-ci2, b-el1, b-el2, b-ipr1, b-ipr2: claude, from the cleaned page images; to be checked by a person.
- Truth of b-su1, b-su2, b-su3: single-question paper: every page is the one answer.
- The weights are set by hand. A random search over 16 of them (400 trials, scored by page Jaccard and start F1 on the six labelled multi-question booklets) reached 0.76 in-sample but only 0.55 leave-one-booklet-out, below the hand-set values (0.75): six booklets are too few to fit weights. Refit once teachers have labelled more booklets.
- The OCR rarely reads the margin labels of the two QP-CI booklets (`5)` read as a word, `10)` as `16)`, `1)` as `7*`), so b-ci1 and b-ci2 lean on similarity and score lowest; the QP-IPR and assignment booklets, whose labels are read, segment well.
- b-ci1's seconds include loading the embedding model (once per process); segmenting a booklet otherwise takes under a second on the CPU.
- b-ipr2 writes 'pg: N' on every page; the OCR read 5 of the 18 numbers (none wrong), under the 40 % needed to reorder, and the upload order was right anyway. No sample has pages out of order, so reordering is tested on synthetic pages only.
